# Immersion and Cinematic Direction

Updated and external sources checked: 2026-09-12. First edition; English adaptation 2026-09-30. Scope: thumbnail review for US English page comics, with left-to-right reading. Reader testing has not been conducted.

## Position of this document

Here, immersion is a production working definition: "Readers can follow the situation with little effort and keep attending to changes in a character's perception, expectations, and emotions." It is not a psychological measurement scale or a complete definition of entertainment. Less explanation is not the sole objective.

- **Confirmed:** the source contains the relevant explanation/example. An instructor's practice is not an experimentally proven universal law.
- **Project policy:** artistic conditions chosen for the readers, format, and project intent.
- **Production suggestion:** a hypothesis developed here to apply and evaluate in actual thumbnails.
- **Unverified:** reader responses or generated results not yet checked in this project.

## Judge as a comic

Choose large panels, silence, backgrounds, and face close-ups according to the information/emotion readers need. Compare adjacent pages instead of prescribing universal frequencies. Suitability for direct film adaptation is not a comic quality criterion.

## From scene to panel script (production suggestion)

1. **State the change in one sentence:** e.g. "Someone who was only waiting accepts the other's absence and decides to leave."
2. **Decide reader knowledge:** learn with the protagonist, or know the danger earlier? Separate intended concealment from information that must remain clear.
3. **Connect place and action:** make the shortest shot sequence that explains who is where, sees what, and does what.
4. **Choose attention:** center each shot on face, hands, interpersonal distance, space, or prop, and state why.
5. **Check the words' job:** duplicate the image, supply absent information, or create a discrepancy with it?
6. **Return to the comic page:** adjust order, early attention, text area, and information after the turn.
7. **Compare a deletion version:** if removing a silent/large panel leaves the intent unchanged, reconsider its purpose.

A panel-script row uses "number / location and characters / framing and distance / visible change / dialogue and sound / reader knowledge / connection forward." These fields need not appear as text in the finished panel.

## Framing and distance review (production suggestion)

"Framing" here broadly describes how to present the view in thumbnails. Record shot size (close/wide) separately from camera angle (high/low).

| Choice | Possible purpose | Failure signs | Possible repair |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Long / wide | Place, escape route, distance, isolation within a group | Essential action unreadable at reduced size | Establish space wide, then show decisive action closer |
| Medium | Follow dialogue and body action together | Repeated chest-up faces; changes exist only in explanation | Show relational change through sitting, standing, or turning away |
| Close-up | Read subtle expression/object change | Unknown hand owner or object location | Establish sleeve, holding hand, or surroundings once beforehand |
| Near-subjective view | Share what a character notices | Readers see something the character could not see | Check eyeline, distance, and obstruction in thumbnails |
| High / low angle | Geography, subjective pressure/vulnerability | Stylish position jumps obscure action | Retain movement landmarks and restore orientation around emphatic angles |
| Hands, feet, back, room | Touch, hesitation, departure, absence | Unclear whether emotion is deliberately hidden or information is simply missing | Specify why the face is withheld and its replacement cue together |

Do not fix correspondences such as "low camera always means power." Judge whose viewpoint it is and its relation to surrounding images.

## Silence, pauses, and tempo (production suggestion)

**Give silent panels something to observe:** fingers before an answer, eyes stopping on a scar, or an empty seat between two people. Make change readable without words. Specify "no dialogue" separately from "no sound."

**A pause is not a duration in seconds.** Small successive panels may read as brief beats or slow readers through more inspection. A sparse large panel can be read quickly. Panel size/count alone cannot determine reading seconds.

**Choose why a face is withheld.** Reducing the effort of devising an expression, delaying an answer, and inviting readers to infer feelings require different substitute images. Many faces are not inherently bad; check whether successive faces show distinct reactions.

**Manage the rarity of strong images.** List candidates for single-panel pages, spreads, and border-breaking characters in the scene plan. If the preceding image has equal intensity, create contrast or state why repetition is intended. There is no universal allowance for a whole work.

| Scene | Information to preserve first | Candidate to try | Misreading to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Everyday life | Spatial continuity, object handling, interpersonal distance | Tidying hands, a room with only everyday sounds | Every silence looks ominous |
| Psychological | Trigger for the emotion | Changed prop handling, space before an answer | Removing even basic clarity about who feels what |
| Battle | Attack origin, target, result | Preparation → omission → landing; close-ups only where needed | Spectacular technique with an unclear target |
| Confession | Speaker/listener information difference | Hands/distance after the line, delayed reply | A visible smile gives away the answer early |
| Explanation | Information needed for the reader's next judgment | Combine explanation with listener action | Mood prevents understanding the rules |

## Limits and adaptations from film (production suggestion)

The following are practical inferences from medium differences. C01's film theory does not itself prove effectiveness in comics.

| Film design | Redesign needed for page comics | Adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shots arrive in fixed order/time | Readers pause, return, and see later images on the same page | Put order-dependent surprises after the turn appropriate to the actual display unit |
| Continuous action in one frame | A still can obscure before/after | Select cause/result and add an intermediate beat if needed |
| Music, ambience, overlapping voices | Silent pages cannot play sound directly | Divide roles among sound-effect lettering, balloons, repeated environmental images |
| Speaker's voice over listener's image | Speaker attribution can become unclear | Establish position first; keep tails/voice distinctions consistent |
| Pan, zoom, tracking | One panel may lose shifting attention | Choose one wide view or separate before/after-discovery panels |
| Long take | More panels do not inherently create continuous time | Retain motion landmarks; omit only changes readers can infer |
| Film-reproducible composition | Comic distortion, metaphor, and text placement have their own roles | Retain expressive border treatment/flair; do not remove it solely for realism |

## Original panel-script example: waiting after closing

Goal: convey a change from waiting to deciding to leave without explanatory thoughts. This is an original example, not an adaptation of a source example.

| Panel | Art and text | Role / connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Wide: a person on a bench outside a closed café; umbrella, streetlight, shop clock; silent | Establish place/waiting. Keep the visible clock time consistent later |
| 2 | Medium: upper-left balloon, "A little longer." The person resettles on the bench | Show an active choice to keep waiting |
| 3 | Close-up: fingers rest on a paper cup without lifting it; no text | Concrete action during waiting, with a reason to withhold the face |
| 4 | Clock through the shop window, later than panel 1; silent | Clarify elapsed time; alternative when a white panel is misread |
| 5 | Feet: the person rises and lifts the umbrella; small sound effect | Show decision through action and connect departure direction to the next panel |
| 6 | Wide: the person leaves; only the bench remains | Resonance; matching panel 1's spatial relationship permits comparison |

Alternative: replace panel 4 with one white panel, reducing specificity of elapsed time. Compare whether that ambiguity suits the intent. Consider enlarging panel 6 only if the wide absence should carry the emotional endpoint; a short event alone is no reason for a full-page panel.

## Writing and verifying review findings

Use "page/panel / intent / current reading / problem / smallest fix / side effects / evidence category." Example: "P2-3, waiting for an answer. The lower-right smile is visible early and reveals the answer. Move it after the turn; page count may increase. Production suggestion."

Priorities 1–5 describe review effort, not quality scores. Report misread character/time/action even at low priority. At high priority, compare alternatives without automatically adding silent panels. Refer to the project's review settings for formal numeric definitions.

For the next reader check, prepare two versions of one scene differing in only one variable. Ask about order confusion, when readers understood what, perceived waiting time, and the most memorable panel. Record preference separately from understanding accuracy. Never promote untested effects to "demonstrated."

## Open questions

- Test silent panels, non-face shots, and white panels under specific genre/audience conditions.
- Optimal splash/large-panel frequency is unknown; judge intent and adjacent contrast.
- When adapting spread design to smartphone single-page viewing or vertical scrolling, redesign around what is simultaneously visible. Japanese right-bound source layouts require explicit reading-direction adaptation too.
- The first edition is usable without purchases. Consider book recommendations/purchases only after free sources and prototypes identify unresolved issues.
