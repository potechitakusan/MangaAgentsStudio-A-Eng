# Comics: Thumbnails, Page Design, and Artwork

Updated / official sources checked: 2026-09-13. The original references cover Japanese right-to-left page comics. This English edition uses US conventions: left-to-right, top-to-bottom panel and balloon order and horizontal English lettering. Design vertical scrolling as a separate display format.

## Production choices in this template

These are production proposals informed by the sources above, not measured laws of effect.

1. **Start with page allocation.** Give readers someone or something to follow at the opening, then reserve space for choices, discoveries, and reactions. Do not force every page to have the same panel count.
2. **Use the full intended text in thumbnails.** Place horizontal English dialogue and internal monologue, normally centered inside balloons, before finding compositions that keep faces, hands, and props visible. Do not finalize fit from text-free roughs alone.
3. **Make the gaps between panels readable.** You can omit each step of climbing stairs while keeping clear who moved where. When skipping before or after an important decision, leave enough clues for readers to bridge the gap.
4. **Give large panels a job.** Record why the space is needed: understanding a setting, emphasizing an entrance, or absorbing an emotion. Size alone does not determine reading time.
5. **Check whether spreads reveal information early.** Record binding, starting position, and single-page or spread display. Odd and even page numbers alone do not establish whether a page-turn reveal works.
6. **Use linework, solid blacks, and tones to distinguish the focal subject.** If characters and backgrounds have similar density, compare simpler backgrounds and white space. More tone is not a substitute for better finishing.
7. **Place sound effects and motion lines as information.** Check the source, direction, intensity, and whose sensation they represent. Also check whether it is acceptable for effects lettering to be read before the dialogue.
8. **Maintain character, prop, and spatial continuity.** Compare faces, hair, clothing, which hand holds each prop, injuries, and left/right positions across panels. Record the panel when a change is intentional.
9. **Review at final size.** Beyond detail visible when zoomed in, check that text, lines, and key evidence remain readable at the intended reading size. Set trim, bleed, and resolution in the project's handoff specifications.
10. **Redesign vertical scrolling screen by screen.** Do not transfer simultaneous spread visibility or page turns unchanged. Check on an actual device where information enters the screen.
11. **Review page shapes and pacing.** When layouts repeat, consider whether the repetition serves the scene. During layout selection, include panel templates that suit the project's approach.
