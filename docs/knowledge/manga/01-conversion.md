# 01 From a Written Comic Script to Panels and Thumbnails

Updated: 2026-09-13. Checks for comic production and review. These are production proposals to select according to the project. For related film and screenwriting material, see [Sources and scope](../manga-sources.md).

<a id="c01"></a>

## C01 Write the script in the order readers experience it

Describe what readers see first, the change they notice next, and the outcome they understand last. Keep background explanations separate from the images and text that actually appear in the panel.

<a id="c02"></a>

## C02 Propose comic panels; map existing shots only when needed

Decide which moment each panel depicts, then number it. Add corresponding shot numbers only when video reference exists. Determine panel count from the information the page needs.

<a id="c03"></a>

## C03 Count shots, units of change, and panels separately

Treat scenes as groups of events, changes as shifts in relationships or decisions, and panels as frames presented to readers. Do not require a one-to-one mapping. Choose an allocation that makes actions and reactions readable.

<a id="c04"></a>

## C04 Split long shots where their meaning changes

Even during continuous action, consider separate panels where an expectation fails, circumstances become clear, or a character changes course. Prioritize the starting point and outcome needed for understanding over every stage of movement.

<a id="c05"></a>

## C05 Combine information only when seeing it together preserves the meaning

When placing several pieces of information in one panel, check that simultaneous visibility preserves the intended effect. Separate anything whose effect would suffer if the outcome caught the eye before its cause.

<a id="c06"></a>

## C06 Specify direction as purpose plus visible content

For a composition, write what readers should understand and what must be visible. This makes alternatives easier to compare during drawing than instructions limited to angle or distance.

<a id="c07"></a>

## C07 Give silence a visible instruction

Even a silent panel needs a subject and a change from the preceding moment. Use expression, posture, or the state of an object to convey the time a character spends waiting, hesitating, or absorbing something.

<a id="c08"></a>

## C08 Convert sound, motion, and inner experience into comic information

Convey motion through before/after poses or motion lines, sound through sound effects or reactions, and inner experience through behavior or necessary internal monologue. Select what understanding requires instead of rendering everything as text.

<a id="c09"></a>

## C09 Distinguish when advice to avoid camera directions applies

A written script handed off for drawing may specify the focal subject, distance, and character placement. Do not transfer film screenplay submission conventions directly into comic restrictions; judge by the needs of thumbnail production.

<a id="c10"></a>

## C10 Check problems that become visible only on the page

Place nearly final text quantities in small thumbnails and check reading order, balloon area, and detail size. Also look for conclusions visible before the intended page turn.
