# 02 Focal Subjects, Framing, Angles, and Viewpoint

Updated: 2026-09-17. Checks for comic production and review. These are production proposals to select according to the project. For related film and screenwriting material, see [Sources and scope](../manga-sources.md).

<a id="v01"></a>

## V01 State the panel's main subject in a few words

Choose one thing readers should notice first. Decide whether the relevant evidence is a face, hand, prop, or space, and reduce competition from text and backgrounds.

<a id="v02"></a>

## V02 Use wider views for context and closer views for evidence

Draw wider when readers need to understand positions or escape routes, closer when they need to read a subtle change. Check whether a change in distance supplies new information.

Choose the order of close and wide views according to what the current panel makes readers want to see next. Following [Setup and answering reader anticipation](03-beat-pacing-timing.md#reader-anticipation), consider whether to direct interest toward an expression or toward the surrounding situation.

<a id="v03"></a>

## V03 Choose angles from the information needed

Choose a viewpoint to reveal height, hide something farther back, or clarify positions. Do not assume high and low angles always have the same psychological effect.

<a id="v04"></a>

## V04 Show recognition through observer, subject, and reaction

Connect the object of a gaze with the reaction after receiving that information. If who learned what matters, show the character's change as well as the observed object.

<a id="v05"></a>

## V05 Show the listener instead of automatically showing the speaker

Sometimes the listener is the one learning something new. Do not automatically choose the person speaking as the next subject. If adding a third person, give them distinct knowledge, expectations, burdens, or judgments. Compare combining reactions if everyone merely shows the same surprise.

When the speaker's words accompany the listener's reaction, make the speaker identifiable. Do not make characters forget information they should know merely to manufacture surprise.

The speaker's position and balloon tails also help identify who is speaking.

<a id="v06"></a>

## V06 Choose reverse views for relationship changes, not just turns in dialogue

You do not need to alternate sides with every line. Change composition when distance closes, control shifts, or another character joins, to show the relationship changing.

<a id="v07"></a>

## V07 Reveal character through backs, walking, and touch

Distribute meaning across gaze, hands, posture, distance, orientation, and leaving or staying instead of concentrating it all in faces. Looking away or turning one's back can imply several emotions; narrow the meaning with the next action. Check whether the face and hands need to repeat the same fact.

Consider hand position, balance, stride, and pressure of contact. When omitting a face, leave other clues from which readers can judge the emotion.

<a id="v08"></a>

## V08 Use obstructions to limit information

Before hiding information behind foreground objects or doors, decide what readers should not see. Check at reduced size that the composition does more than make characters hard to see.

<a id="v09"></a>

## V09 Shape experience by moving between objective and subjective views

Distinguish views that establish overall positions from views limited to what a character sees. Place gaze or position cues before and after subjective views to make the viewpoint's owner clear.

<a id="v10"></a>

## V10 Give unoccupied views an independent purpose

Find runs of panels with the same distance, angle, or focal subject, then judge whether the repetition is needed to read changing expressions or recurring actions. If place, time, or object state is unclear, compare the current version with wider views, hands, or background changes. More background can dilute tension or intimacy, so do not impose fixed rules such as a background every few panels or a maximum number of faces.

For boarding, alighting, entrances, and exits, follow starting position → movement → destination. Check connections among orientation, hands, belongings, and doors. The actual images must be traceable, not merely connected by explanatory prose.

Production proposal added 2026-09-19. See [Sources for production improvements](../manga-sources.md#web-workflow-20260919) for inspiration and scope. Reader effects from adjusting visual density are unverified.

When using a background without characters, assign a role such as elapsed time, absence, or changed circumstances. If nothing changes, reconsider whether that background is needed to connect adjacent panels.

<a id="v11"></a>

## V11 Repeat a composition to make differences readable

When reusing a composition, preserve differences you want readers to compare, such as character positions or object states. Check whether the first composition is memorable, including the interval before it repeats.

<a id="v12"></a>

## V12 Build meaning from adjacent images

Pair the expectation created by one panel with the information supplied by the next. Even attractive individual images need a continuous read to check for confused causality or viewpoint when placed together.

Check whether later images or dialogue answer [questions and expectations created by the setup](03-beat-pacing-timing.md#reader-anticipation). Compare a reversed order to see whether it loses the reason for curiosity or discovery.

<a id="v13"></a>

## V13 Preserve spatial continuity in the written script

Record characters' left/right positions, orientations, entrances, exits, and which hands hold tools for each scene. If reversing orientation, insert an image that establishes movement or a new angle.

For image generation, also align the focal subject, how much of a face is visible, before/after action, and contact points to preserve. Compare these against panels and pages after generation, cropping, and finishing. Check for changed intent when an eye-only crop becomes a full face, a deliberately hidden face is filled in, or the leading part of an action is cropped away. Operational steps are in [External image generation C1–C5](../external-panel-i2i.md#c1). Added 2026-09-26 as a production proposal; this does not break the overall assessment in [Production evidence E3](../image-generation/production-evidence.md#e3) into measured effects of individual framing choices.

<a id="v14"></a>

## V14 Check at reduced size that important things look important

Display the PNG at its intended publication size and check whether key expressions and props are discoverable. Readability only when enlarged is not enough to mark the artwork complete.
