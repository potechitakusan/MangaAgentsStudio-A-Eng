# 06 Serials, Ensemble Casts, and Comedy

Updated: 2026-09-13. Checks for comic production and review. These are production proposals to select according to the project. For related film and screenwriting material, see [Sources and scope](../manga-sources.md).

<a id="l01"></a>

## L01 Describe what the comic does each episode with a verb

Express repeatable actions or challenges as verbs. Try several episode ideas to check that the premise can keep creating new choices and outcomes, beyond merely reusing people and places.

<a id="l02"></a>

## L02 Separate an episode's resolution from continuing conflict

Distinguish the answer delivered in this episode from the challenge carried forward. Check for hooks that resolve nothing and endings that offer no reason to read on.

<a id="l03"></a>

## L03 Build a cast whose strengths also cause problems

Design relationships where one person's skillful behavior creates trouble for another. Leave room for experience to alter relationships slightly instead of repeating the same argument every time.

<a id="l04"></a>

## L04 Establish main and secondary threads before interweaving them

Give each thread a beginning, change, and result before alternating them. Check how the thread you cut to resonates with the preceding question or emotion.

<a id="l05"></a>

## L05 Use transitions for time skips and contrast

Decide what time can pass while following another character and what information the switch should contrast. On returning, make location, time, and purpose easy to recover.

<a id="l06"></a>

## L06 Use several kinds of hooks

Hooks can arise from decisions, discoveries, promises, and relationship changes as well as danger. Show what readers can anticipate next without taking away the previous episode's payoff.

<a id="l07"></a>

## L07 Distinguish ensemble reactions and interests

Let different characters gain and lose different things from the same event. When sequencing reactions, prioritize characters who supply new information.

<a id="l08"></a>

## L08 Generate comedy from premise and character

Build humor from mismatches between a character's desires or assumptions and the situation. Check that a one-off pun has not displaced the scene's purpose.

<a id="l09"></a>

## L09 Make readers encounter setup, development, and punch line in order

Provide the information needed to understand the punch line first so readers can form an expectation. If punch-line text is visible too early on the page, change the layout.

<a id="l10"></a>

## L10 Return to the setup when the joke is weak

When the joke does not land, check what readers expected before making the punch line more spectacular. Fix insufficient explanation separately from excessive predictability.

<a id="l11"></a>

## L11 Update the meaning of recurring jokes

Change the target, situation, or result of a recurring joke. Check whether familiarity with the earlier event contributes to the next joke.

<a id="l12"></a>

## L12 Match comedy targets and serious moments to the characters

Choose who becomes the butt of a joke and where serious issues occur in keeping with the work's characterization. Read across adjacent scenes to check for unintended cancellation of a serious scene's emotion.
