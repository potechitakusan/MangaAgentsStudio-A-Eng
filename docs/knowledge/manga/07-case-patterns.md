# 07 Staging Patterns to Try in Comics

Updated: 2026-09-13. Production proposals for choosing comic techniques by intended effect. Compare them in thumbnails for each project rather than reproducing scenes from films or television. Reader effects have not been measured. See [Related sources and scope](../manga-sources.md).

| Aim | Possible comic depiction | What to compare or check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Show a departure from the perspective of the person left behind | Entryway before departure → empty road → fewer shoes | Is the omitted event understandable? Would showing the departure itself fit better? |
| Leave an emotional aftermath | A hallway after a conversation, an empty seat, dishes left unwashed | Whose absence or what change does the setting convey? |
| Show two people unable to say what they mean | An argument over returning or refusing to accept a cup | Can readers infer the real dispute from the struggle over the prop? |
| Show a relationship changing | Repeat the same dining table while changing only people, distance, and gazes | Is the initial composition memorable? Does repetition add meaning? |
| Distinguish interests in an ensemble | Announcement → someone calculating → someone looking at a neighbor → someone leaving | Does this avoid repeating identical surprised faces? |
| Reverse the force of words | A phrase used jokingly at first, then repeated to an expressionless listener | Can readers remember it? Do the image and reaction alter its meaning? |
| Resolve a relationship physically | Grip an offered hand, rest a hand on it, or decline to return the grip | Are differences in position and pressure readable at publication size? |
| Convey tension around news that has not reached its recipient | Ask for repetition → hesitate over whom to tell → deliver the message | Are uncertain information and confirmed facts kept distinct? |
| Connect closeness to a later loss | Include temporary cooperation or enjoyment of a shared task | Would removing the respite also remove the contrast with the later section? |
| Convey the richness of a shared past | Detail the relationship's beginning, then skip later periods through changes in the same object | Could readers misread the elapsed years? Do not fix allocation by percentage |
| Let a character see their future in others | A protagonist watches another pair, then turns back toward their own partner | Is there a connection between what they see and their decision? |
| Make images and narration disagree | Show actual behavior, then the character's face-saving narration | Can readers distinguish the narrator's misunderstanding from the work's established facts? |
| Interrupt grand words with everyday life | Chores continue beside a confession; hands stop just once | Does the interruption change emotion or information? |
| End on something that did not happen | Prepare a confession → the opportunity closes → return to routine | Can readers recognize the event they had been expecting? |
| Make multiple times or memories readable | Change objects or frame treatment in the same location, then return to the present | Can readers follow whose memory it is and the present emotional focus? |
| Change the way a character addresses readers | Address them → avoid them → finally turn back | Distinguish ordinary internal monologue from direct address to readers |

Do not turn psychological effects of composition, proportions of omitted events, or counts of internal monologue into universal rules.
