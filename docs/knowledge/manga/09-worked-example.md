# 09 Compare Thumbnails with Different Panel Allocations

Updated: 2026-09-13. A comic production proposal: a procedure for comparing several versions of one scene, not a sample finished work.

## Fix these before comparing

- The scene's beginning and end, character goals, and outcome.
- Information readers should receive and information that should not appear early.
- Finalized dialogue and the feeling to leave at the end.

## Vary panel allocation

First make a version that communicates all required information. Next make versions that separate actions and reactions, or combine information that can be seen simultaneously. Even with a target panel count, decide which functions to preserve before fitting the number.

| Comparison | Check in the separated version | Check in the combined version |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Situation and action | Is establishing the situation taking too long? | Can character positions and actions be read together? |
| Speech and reaction | Does time spent reading the reaction serve a purpose? | Could speakers and listeners be confused? |
| Discovery and judgment | Is the process of noticing and thinking necessary? | Does the next action feel abrupt? |
| Result and emotional aftermath | Is there an image that absorbs the change? | Does the scene end before the result can be understood? |

## Check on the page

Insert nearly final text quantities and read both versions at the intended publication size. Compare reading order, rereading, early reveals, detail size, and connections to adjacent pages. Do not assume fewer panels mean faster reading; consider textual and visual density as well.

Record the reason for selection and lost effects in the thumbnail review. Organize these as feedback only when asked to record feedback.
