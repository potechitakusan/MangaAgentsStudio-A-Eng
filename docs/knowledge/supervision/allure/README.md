# Supervision: Non-Explicit Allure and Moments of Awareness

Added: 2026-09-19. Experimental. Design moments of becoming aware of another person through gaze, gestures, distance, and relationships, without nudity or sexual acts. Confirm ages, relationships, and the work's expressive boundaries from existing settings. Do not add adult sexual implications to characters whose age is unknown.

The main direct sources explain single illustrations. Applying them to sequential comic scenes is this kit's production proposal, informed by the [Source register](../sources.md) and [Common knowledge](../../review-workflow.md#common-knowledge); it does not guarantee that readers will perceive allure. Select A01–A06 according to characterization and scene intent.

## Decide first

Distinguish whether the aim is to make a character appealing to readers, make a character become aware of someone else, or change the relationship between both people. Check whether the person consciously displays their appeal or is unaware of it. Do not automatically equate romance, respect, comfort, and tension.

<a id="a01"></a>

## A01 Who notices what?

- Examine: Are the observer, object of attention, and moment of noticing identifiable? Are a reader-facing composition and a character's viewpoint being confused?
- Separate the observed person's intent from the observer's interpretation. One person being captivated does not establish that the other is inviting attention or feels affection.
- Use when: An appealing image exists, but it is unclear who became aware of the other person.
- Compare: Change one action by the observer, clarify the object of the gaze, or treat the image as a showcase for the character alone.
- Costs and exceptions: Adding a reaction may narrow reader interpretation. A character showcase does not require an in-story spectator.
- Basis: [S06](../sources.md#s06) informs gaze in a single illustration. Connecting it to an observer applies common viewpoint knowledge.

<a id="a02"></a>

## A02 Place an unusual moment inside the ongoing task

- Examine: Are ordinary behavior, the current change, and its reason clear? Do seriousness, hesitation, or consideration arise from the character's purpose?
- Use when: Added blushing or close-ups do not clarify how the perception of the person changes.
- Compare: Stop a task that normally continues, replace an almost-spoken joke with another reply, or notice the other person's work.
- Costs and exceptions: A forced contrast breaks consistency. For someone who habitually displays their appeal, sustained composure may fit better than a change.
- Basis: Adapted from [S04](../sources.md#s04), [S07](../sources.md#s07), and [S08](../sources.md#s08). These do not establish proven effects of allure.

<a id="a03"></a>

## A03 Give gestures and distance a reason in the scene

- Examine: Why does the hand move or the person approach, and how does the other receive it? Does use of clothing, tools, and space fit the established character?
- Use when: An unnatural approach or pose exists solely for a showcase.
- Compare: Focus on a handoff during shared work, meet gazes at a distance, or begin approaching and wait for the other person's response.
- Costs and exceptions: Too many detailed daily actions can stall the task. Greater closeness may read as pressure instead of intimacy. Use the other person's choices as clues.
- Basis: Gestures in [S05](../sources.md#s05) and [S06](../sources.md#s06). The interaction design is this kit's proposal.

<a id="a04"></a>

## A04 Show a small choice after noticing

- Examine: After looking at the other person, what changes in reply, hands, gaze, posture, or handling of the task? Is the reaction's cause identifiable?
- Use when: Feelings are explained, but awareness of the other person does not affect behavior.
- Compare: Rephrase a reply, look at the person a moment after a handoff, or keep working while pretending composure. Also compare retaining internal monologue.
- Costs and exceptions: More silence or blushing does not establish affection. Surprise, fatigue, or discomfort may also explain it. If ambiguity is intended, do not treat it as a defect.
- Basis: [S04](../sources.md#s04), [S07](../sources.md#s07), and existing performance knowledge.

<a id="a05"></a>

## A05 Select a focus without reducing appeal to bodily shorthand

- Examine: Which expression, hand movement, clothing detail, or posture does the observer notice now? Is appeal being explained only through gender or a body part?
- Use when: More detail has made it harder to know what to look at.
- Compare: Move from a readable action to a detail, select one detail, or convey the effect through distance and gaze without a close-up.
- Costs and exceptions: Close details can obscure spatial orientation or whose action or viewpoint is being shown. Reducing anatomical depiction is not itself the goal. Do not make the sources' clothing, hairstyles, or physical features mandatory.
- Basis: Considering focal points with reference to [S05](../sources.md#s05) and [S06](../sources.md#s06).

<a id="a06"></a>

## A06 Preserve both the lingering feeling and the scene's business

- Examine: How does awareness change the subsequent reply or choice? Does it compete with a main effect such as comedy, danger, or farewell?
- Use when: Added mutual gazing stops necessary conversation or removes seriousness.
- Compare: Make a small change while preserving the reply to the task, move the showcase elsewhere, or do not intensify it this time.
- Costs and exceptions: Accelerating the relationship can lose the interest of a not-yet-aware stage. Do not mechanically turn a serious response into embarrassment or comedy.
- Basis: [S08](../sources.md#s08) and existing knowledge of main effects and scene changes.

## Questions for comparing approaches

| Comparison | Hold constant | Difference to examine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Showcase for readers / an in-story character captivated | Character and action | Whose feeling is conveyed? |
| Conscious / unaware | Same task and relationship | Does it look intentionally inviting? |
| Approach / maintain distance | Interest in the other person | Does it read as intimacy, hesitation, or pressure? |
| Show the reaction / leave interpretation open | Events and settings supplied | Clarity of awareness versus lingering ambiguity |

Do not change unrelated conditions at the same time. Do not use this supervision to intensify scenes by adding sexual exposure or acts. Do not change an established absence of affection into romance for supervisory convenience.

## Feedback and verification

Return target location / visible fact / interpretation hypothesis / A number / proposed change / expected effect and cost. Dialogue or a written script cannot verify subtle expressions or distance in finished images.

In PNGs, check at publication size whether the object of a gaze, space between people, and meaning of gestures are readable. In trial reads, first ask who seemed aware of what and which detail suggested it, then ask about appeal and taste. Record readings of admiration or respect as such; do not relabel them as allure. Return to [Common review](../../review-workflow.md).
