# Supervision: Anger, Arguments, and Rupture

Status: Experimental. Updated / sources checked: 2026-09-19. Covers what an angry person wants and how words or actions change relationships. These are **production proposals**, not guidance for diagnosing or managing real anger. Use them to make fictional emotions and choices readable.

Main sources: [S10](../sources.md#s10) and [S13](../sources.md#s13); for character-specific expressions, [S09](../sources.md#s09). Compare conflicting beliefs, anger that conveys more than fear, and individual expressions. Consult [Common scene and dialogue knowledge](../../manga/04-scene-dialogue-props.md) and [Straight-man responses](../tsukkomi/README.md) when needed.

<a id="r01"></a>

## R01 Separate the target of anger from the change sought

- Examine: Whose action prompts the response, and what should the other person stop, admit, or protect? Separate the character's account from what actually happened.
- Use when: Shouting is evident but the dispute is not.
- Compare: Show the triggering act, show the same words meaning different things to different people, or narrow the real issue through a reaction to a reply.
- Costs and exceptions: Do not always add history or circumstances that justify anger. Displacement, misunderstanding, and jealousy are also possible. Understanding a reason differs from endorsing an act.
- Basis: S13's connection between values and emotions, and common knowledge of desires and attempts to influence others.

<a id="r02"></a>

## R02 Give the continuing argument a reason

- Examine: Where do known facts, expectations, or nonnegotiable values fail to align? Separate claims from established story facts; do not relabel falsehood as a mere difference in values. Does the exchange do more than repeat the same position after every reply?
- Use when: An easily clarified misunderstanding becomes a long argument without reason.
- Compare: Values remain incompatible after facts are known, lost trust prevents an explanation from reaching the listener, or a reply differs from the answer sought.
- Costs and exceptions: Both people having reasons does not make their responsibility symmetrical. If simple hostility or one-sided rejection is intended, do not force a righteous cause onto both.
- Basis: Applying S10's individual beliefs and contrasts to conversational progression.

<a id="r03"></a>

## R03 Preserve individual voice and stages of anger

- Examine: How do phrasing, forms of address, reply length, hands, and position differ from ordinary behavior? Is an intense expression appearing before its basis?
- Use when: Everyone has the same shouting face, or imposing “quiet anger” weakens individuality.
- Compare: Raise the voice, revert to formal speech, rephrase, or stop a task. Select with the surrounding responses.
- Costs and exceptions: Keep shouting or tears when they suit the person. Small changes must be distinguishable in PNGs; writing them in the script does not establish them visually.
- Basis: Character-specific emotion in S09 and S13, plus common performance and reaction knowledge.

<a id="r04"></a>

## R04 Show a crossed boundary through subsequent behavior

- Examine: How does the line or act change entrusting, touching, contacting, or staying together?
- Use when: An apparent rupture resets in the next panel, or the scene only intensifies an argument.
- Compare: End the conversation, stop a shared role, act separately, or rephrase to avoid rupture.
- Costs and exceptions: Ending the relationship is an allowed outcome. Reducing anger or achieving apology and reconciliation is not the supervisor's goal. Adding irreversible hurt changes conditions for later developments.
- Basis: Applying common before/after scene changes and character choices to anger and rupture.

<a id="r05"></a>

## R05 Check the recipient's choice and the boundary with humor

- Examine: Does the recipient refuse, listen, answer back, leave, or do something else? Has added humor erased the original issue?
- Use when: Moving toward banter or embarrassment makes serious rejection appear trivial.
- Compare: Place a small joke after the response, move humor elsewhere, or preserve pain and disagreement.
- Costs and exceptions: Nonresponse can be a choice, but do not assume agreement or resolution. In relationships with habitual fierce arguments, strong language alone does not prove rupture.
- Basis: S10, S13, and existing straight-man response supervision. These do not establish that anger always comes from affection.

## Comparison, images, and trial reads

Keep the dispute and outcome constant while comparing shouting / restraint or replying / ending the conversation. In PNGs, check who speaks to whom and what information changes an attitude. Do not uniformly rewrite everyone into the calm style you prefer.

In trial reads, first ask what the argument concerned, how the relationship changed by the end, and which details suggested that. Ask separately about empathy, fear, and persuasiveness. Follow [Common supervision procedures](../routing.md) to separate observations from hypotheses and acceptance from costs. Comparative verification with actual project PNGs and readers has not been performed.
