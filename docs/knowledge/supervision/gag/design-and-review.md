# Comedy Idea Development and Supervision

Compiled: 2026-09-17. Production proposals for written scripts and previews. Use alongside the [Mechanism index](mechanisms.md). No prescribed panel counts or layouts are included.

## Develop ideas in the written script

1. Decide the main effect and the emotions and relationships to preserve when adding humor. If comedy is not primary, strengthening it is not a goal in itself.
2. Write the premise readers initially believe and the answer they would ordinarily expect. Check that the story provides grounds for that expectation.
3. Choose the dimension to shift: goal, evaluation, interpretation, condition, degree, or role. Do not add new settings or personality changes solely to enable the mismatch.
4. Check that the actual answer fits prior facts and arises from character desires. Decide whether images, dialogue, or actions supply the needed evidence.
5. Choose the recipient's response according to this character. Options include establishing a normal baseline, evaluating differently, amplifying the mismatch, revealing their own bias, accepting it, reacting silently, or omitting a response.
6. Trim explanation after the answer is understood. Compare the target of the joke, the impression of the character, and the effect on the scene's main purpose.

For desires, mistaken beliefs, strengths and weaknesses, and knowledge differences, use [Character](../../manga/05-story-character.md), [Performance](../../manga/04-scene-dialogue-props.md), and [Information and pauses](../../manga/03-beat-pacing-timing.md).

## Use character and repetition

The comic instigator and straight-man responder (boke and tsukkomi) are functions in the moment, not fixed personalities. If a usual corrector becomes biased about a personal interest, let readers recognize the reason for the role change. Give a third character different knowledge, interests, or stakes instead of adding another restatement of the same correction.

For repetition, examine what changes in the target, position, recipient, degree, result, or self-awareness, beyond simply doing the same thing again. Even an expected action can offer enjoyment through its execution or new consequences. Identify portions that depend on knowing the existing relationship and leave clues for first-time readers.

## Inspect the preview

Open the actual target images and identify version, page, and location. Select items below where a failure is suspected; do not mechanically fill out every item.

- Are the premise and ordinary expectation readable? Is simple confusion being treated as surprise?
- Is the evidence establishing the mismatch visible? Are necessary subjects or conditions missing?
- If the title, expression, or dialogue reveals the answer early, is there still something enjoyable afterward?
- Does the recipient's reaction add meaning, or explain again what is already readable?
- Does the punch line undo a serious emotion, the value of a resolution, or another person's choice?
- Does the change suddenly make a character look incompetent or uncaring without a causal explanation?

“Understandable” and “funny” are separate. Do not record a supervisor's hypothesis as an actual reader response. Return revisions as location / observed fact / interpretation hypothesis / proposed change / effects preserved and lost. Use [Common review](../../review-workflow.md) when needed.
