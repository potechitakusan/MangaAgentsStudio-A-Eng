# Mechanisms of Humor: 23 Options

Compiled: 2026-09-17. Each item offers a structure and production proposal for building humor. These do not guarantee joke strength; choose what suits these characters, this situation, and the intended feeling afterward.

Review in this order: **premise → expected answer → actual answer → clues for understanding the difference**. This describes information relationships, not assignments to specific panels. Select what is needed; do not use everything.

## G01 Replace the goal

- Structure: A character pursuing an original task repeatedly acts for pleasure or personal gain, pushing the task itself aside.
- Production proposal: Separate the stated purpose from the desire the character cannot help prioritizing. End with a result that changes how readers understand what the character was working toward.
- Check: Do readers remember the original task? Is the detour connected to character desire rather than merely off-topic?

## G02 Evaluate the same fact by a different standard

- Structure: What one person sees as danger or burden, another values for profit, convenience, or rarity.
- Production proposal: Preserve the event and change only the standard for good or bad.
- Check: Is differing evaluation being confused with lack of knowledge? Is what each person values clear?

## G03 Take a metaphor or idiom literally

- Structure: A figurative explanation receives a physical interpretation in the reply or action.
- Production proposal: Use an expression whose ordinary reading comes naturally, then show the other interpretation through action or outcome.
- Check: Does the ambiguity already exist in the language? Is the dialogue unnatural solely to make the joke work?

## G04 Fulfill a promise narrowly

- Structure: Words inviting an expectation of cooperation or assurance satisfy only the minimum literal meaning or a limited condition.
- Production proposal: Use the gap between the expectation readers supply and what the statement actually guarantees.
- Check: Is the listener's expectation reasonable? Does the later development violate something the original statement explicitly promised?

## G05 Change the object of praise or concern

- Structure: Words seemingly praising or worrying about a person or achievement turn out to concern a different target.
- Production proposal: Establish the emotional direction first, then identify its actual target.
- Check: Is the new target immediately clear? Are vague pronouns merely confusing readers?

## G06 Reinterpret the motive for kindness

- Structure: An apparently altruistic act turns out to offer a specific benefit to its performer.
- Production proposal: Keep the kind act real and change its meaning through an additional private motive.
- Check: Must the character become cruel? Have you considered a version where goodwill and self-interest coexist?

## G07 Miss a condition required for success

- Structure: Someone thinks they copied a method, followed advice, or prepared everything, but a necessary condition is absent.
- Production proposal: Show an apparently correct attempt, then reveal why something expected to work cannot.
- Check: Is a new prohibition invented only at the end? Are there clues that make the oversight understandable?

## G08 Let the solution undermine the goal

- Structure: Solving the immediate problem consumes resources or conditions needed for the ultimate goal.
- Production proposal: Connect a locally rational solution to the overall loss it creates.
- Check: Does the loss result from the action? Is unrelated bad luck being added afterward?

## G09 Mismatch scale, time, or effort

- Structure: An excellent method takes too long, preparation for a trivial task is excessive, or the response to a major crisis is too small.
- Production proposal: Keep problem and response aligned in direction while shifting degree or time.
- Check: Are the two quantities being compared clear? Does the joke rely only on the size of the exaggeration?

## G10 Reverse roles or relative strength

- Structure: The protector needs protection, the restraining person loses composure, or someone apparently weak solves the problem immediately.
- Production proposal: Establish the expected role first, then provide a specific ability, circumstance, or desire that changes it.
- Check: Has ability or personality changed merely for the punch line? Is the reason for the reversal readable?

## G11 Let actions contradict words

- Structure: A confident explanation, noble claim, or general principle appears close to behavior or a result that contradicts it.
- Production proposal: Use words for self-image and images or action for reality.
- Check: Is dialogue explaining the contradiction twice? Is enough evidence present for readers to compare?

## G12 Bring grand authority down to an everyday scale

- Structure: A dignified or grand person or event is handled according to familiar habits or practical concerns.
- Production proposal: Establish the grandeur, then introduce a small concern anyone can understand.
- Check: Is there a reason the everyday concern arises here? Does it do more than break the setting?

## G13 Cancel a premise supplied by the reader

- Structure: Readers readily infer an unstated circumstance, then a later arrival or fact disproves that inference.
- Production proposal: Make the same earlier information compatible with both the expected and actual explanations.
- Check: Has the narration lied? Does it still work when reread with the answer known?

## G14 Reveal costs or side effects immediately after success

- Structure: After a solution, acquisition, or act of help succeeds, an accompanying burden or other problem appears.
- Production proposal: Preserve success and add what it cost.
- Check: Must success lose all its value? Have you compared a version that preserves the character's sense of achievement?

## G15 Correct the claim without offering reassurance

- Structure: A denial corrects only incidental attributes, leaving the core concern intact or revealing a larger problem.
- Production proposal: Separate the correction the straight-man responder wants from the part the other person wants to correct.
- Check: Is what remains before and after correction clear? Is this more than repeating the same denial?

## G16 Let a normally helpful act backfire because of the recipient's circumstances

- Structure: An ordinarily helpful action has the opposite effect because of the recipient's knowledge, caution, or purpose.
- Production proposal: Give the same act different meanings for sender and recipient.
- Check: Is the recipient's interpretation grounded? Does the scene avoid ending in unexplained failure?

## G17 Import the common sense of a different setting

- Structure: Familiar habits or past experience guide judgment of a new system, person, or situation.
- Production proposal: Establish a rule that works by the character's standard but not in the current setting.
- Check: Is the difference between the two rules readable? Is unexplained specialist knowledge required?

## G18 Overuse a strength

- Structure: Expertise, thoroughness, or powerful ability creates unnecessary work or extra damage.
- Production proposal: Preserve the strength while misapplying its scope or degree.
- Check: Does the failure occur because the character is good at something, rather than suddenly bad at it?

## G19 Give a seemingly useless answer another value

- Structure: Even without answering the question directly, another skill or offering advances the goal. Sometimes only other people recognize its value.
- Production proposal: Begin with a low evaluation, then show usefulness on another dimension.
- Check: Is an unrelated all-purpose ability being added? Can the initial negative assessment coexist with later usefulness?

## G20 Reinterpret the cause

- Structure: An outcome apparently caused by a major external force turns out to come from a small voluntary action or familiar cause.
- Production proposal: Fix the symptom or result first, then change the expected explanation.
- Check: Does the actual cause connect to the result? Has an explicitly established cause been changed without justification?

## G21 Shift the priority of the reaction instead of the problem

- Structure: Saving face, a minor convenience, or effort receives concern before a major crisis or loss.
- Production proposal: Preserve the event and reveal values through what the character reacts to.
- Check: Does this fit the intended characterization, including the possibility of appearing uncaring?

## G22 Add a second mismatch after correction

- Structure: Even after the first misunderstanding is resolved, the corrector's desire or a new condition creates another problem.
- Production proposal: Let the later mismatch arise from the initial action, correction, or personality.
- Check: Does the extra surprise cancel the earlier discovery? Are unrelated jokes increasing the burden of understanding?

## G23 Undercut an image of a character using only results or traces

- Structure: A small remaining result reveals clumsiness, a mundane or worldly side, or another desire beneath a reputation or impressive appearance.
- Production proposal: Let readers revise their view from evidence of behavior rather than explanation.
- Check: Is the evidence visibly connected to that person or action? Is the character being diminished on speculation alone?
