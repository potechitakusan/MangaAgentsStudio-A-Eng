# Supervision: Grief, Loss, and Aftermath

Status: Experimental. Updated / sources checked: 2026-09-19. Covers absent people or places, lost relationships or opportunities, and characters living afterward. These are **production proposals**, not medical stages of grief or a prescribed recovery process.

Main sources: [S14](../sources.md#s14) and [S15](../sources.md#s15). Compare an author depicting personal experience with one depicting researched places and absence. For pauses, props, and endings, return to common knowledge in [Pacing and timing](../../manga/03-beat-pacing-timing.md), [Scenes, dialogue, and props](../../manga/04-scene-dialogue-props.md), and [Story and character](../../manga/05-story-character.md).

<a id="g01"></a>

## G01 Examine what was lost and what is no longer possible

- Examine: Was the loss a person, place, or future? What can no longer happen in the relationship, and what does the character still want to continue?
- Use when: Sadness is explained but the individual's particular loss is unreadable.
- Compare: Briefly establish an earlier role, show an action now impossible, or leave something still needing to be said.
- Costs and exceptions: No complete relationship history is required. Do not turn disappearance or separation into confirmed death; preserve what characters and readers respectively know.
- Basis: Combining S14 and S15 with common knowledge of desire and knowledge differences.

<a id="g02"></a>

## G02 Convey absence through continuing habits and changed procedures

- Examine: Can a familiar object or action no longer carry its former meaning? Is its connection to the absent person readable?
- Use when: An empty seat or keepsake alone fails to convey the absence's meaning.
- Compare: Hands stop during the same task, a routine changes for one fewer person, or the character chooses to put something away or keep it.
- Costs and exceptions: Overexplaining symbolism narrows the emotional aftermath. A newly introduced object can acquire a role through present action or a short line. If its origin is unknown, show that uncertainty or the character's imagined explanation. Do not invent past intimacy; distinguish imagination from confirmed fact. Do not require a long flashback first.
- Basis: S15's approach to sensing time through objects at a location, and S14's depiction of daily life.

<a id="g03"></a>

## G03 Preserve personal reactions and mixed emotions

- Examine: Do tears, anger, laughter, hunger, focus on work, or delayed response connect to this person and this moment?
- Use when: A character who does not cry is judged insufficiently sad, or everyone is made to react alike.
- Compare: Crying, being unable to cry, handling a task, or laughing briefly. Compare alongside the next choice.
- Costs and exceptions: Quiet depiction is not inherently superior. Open sobbing may fit. Laughing or eating alone does not mean grief has disappeared.
- Basis: S14's experience and creative approach. Do not generalize one author's experience into universal responses or recovery stages.

<a id="g04"></a>

## G04 Choose when understanding occurs and when emotion appears

- Examine: What changes the reaction: receiving news, touching a familiar object, or being left alone?
- Use when: The only option considered is a crying face immediately after explanation, or silent panels repeat the same state.
- Compare: Absorb the news immediately, pause after handling a task, or skip time and show a changed action.
- Costs and exceptions: Delayed tears are not always deeper. Keep repetition or stagnation when intended. Check pause length through pages, publication format, and trial reads.
- Basis: S14, S15, and common treatment of reactions and time.

<a id="g05"></a>

## G05 Separate the scene's ending from the end of grief

- Examine: How far does this scene close its task or choice, and what remains unresolved? Is a small resumed action being substituted for full recovery or forgiveness?
- Use when: A bright line supposedly resolves the loss, or a pursuit of lingering feeling removes a necessary ending.
- Compare: Finish only today's task, change one habit, choose a next action without an answer, or provide intermediate clues when depicting recovery.
- Costs and exceptions: Removing hope or solace is not a goal either. Follow the work's settings for people who find release in parting or retain conflicting feelings.
- Basis: S14's reassessment over time, S15's challenge to easy declarations of closure, and common ending knowledge.

## Comparison, images, and trial reads

Keep loss, elapsed time, and relationships constant. Compare tears / action, flashback / present traces, or stating / leaving unsaid. In PNGs, check objects connected to absence, where movement stops, and temporal differences. Enlarging a symbol alone does not guarantee meaning.

In trial reads, ask what was lost and what changed or remained at the end. Do not judge success solely by whether readers cried. Follow [Common supervision procedures](../routing.md) to record acceptance and costs. Comparative verification with actual project PNGs and readers has not been performed.
