# Supervision: Unease and Fear

Status: Experimental. Updated / sources checked: 2026-09-19. Covers a sense that something is wrong, approaching threats, and events not fully understood. These are **production proposals**; distinguish fear intended by the author from fear actually felt by readers.

Main sources: [S11](../sources.md#s11) and [S12](../sources.md#s12). Compare uses of light, shadow, and linework with creators' methods for translating sound and uncertainty into comics. For knowledge differences and presentation order, see [Common pacing and timing knowledge](../../manga/03-beat-pacing-timing.md).

<a id="h01"></a>

## H01 Make the departure from ordinary life readable

- Examine: Is the normal state for this world or person clear, along with what differs now? Do only readers notice, or do characters notice too?
- Use when: An eerie object is present but its difference from the setting's ordinary life is unclear.
- Compare: Changes in the same location, a familiar procedure failing, or an anomaly nobody responds to. Choose comparable clues.
- Costs and exceptions: Lengthy added normality slows an opening. Even a world strange from the outset can offer footing through local rules or character responses.
- Basis: Adapted from S11 and S12 on creating a sense of wrongness.

<a id="h02"></a>

## H02 Separate what is concealed from what readers need to follow

- Examine: How much has been supplied about place, the person followed, events, and signs of danger? Is an unknown identity being confused with an unreadable image?
- Use when: Excessive concealment obscures the situation, or excessive explanation removes imaginative space.
- Compare: Show only the result, reveal part of the appearance, or show the whole while hiding motives or the next action.
- Costs and exceptions: Revealing the entity can also create fear. Concealment is not always scarier. If solving a mystery is the aim, preserve evidence needed for later understanding.
- Basis: Combining S12's challenge of making things concrete in comics with common information-presentation knowledge.

<a id="h03"></a>

## H03 Change expectation or danger while readers wait

- Examine: Across quiet panels, does the sound's location, the remaining options, or the character's understanding change?
- Use when: Repeated dark scenery obscures the reason for waiting.
- Compare: Repeat a sign with one change, insert an attempt to investigate, or jump abruptly to the result.
- Costs and exceptions: Added warning can remove sudden surprise. For page-turn effects, check whether spreads, single-page display, or vertical scrolling expose later information early.
- Basis: Applying the common principle “the longer the wait, the more expectation or danger should change” to fear. No specific panel count or reading duration has empirical support here.

<a id="h04"></a>

## H04 Connect light, linework, and sound to clues of disturbance

- Examine: Why choose black, white, line density, or the presence or absence of effects lettering? Are intended clues buried?
- Use when: Revision merely darkens the background or adds detail unrelated to fear.
- Compare: Grouped blacks, one unsettling detail in a bright setting, or sound conveyed through words, reactions, or visual density. Compare the information each scene needs.
- Costs and exceptions: Abstraction alone may obscure what happened. Shadow, silence, and fine lines are all optional. Do not instruct reproduction of a source's motifs or linework.
- Basis: Production methods in S11 and S12. One illustration of S12's sound scene was visually inspected; the full surrounding sequence and general reader responses remain unverified.

<a id="h05"></a>

## H05 Preserve why characters fear and what they do

- Examine: Do personal knowledge, what they do not want to lose, and reasons to flee, investigate, or stay connect?
- Use when: Fear is intensified by making someone approach a known danger without reason, or by giving everyone the same response.
- Compare: An anomaly surviving rational investigation, an unexpected result of action, or contrasts between composed and shaken people.
- Costs and exceptions: Do not fix irrationality when it is intentional. If time pressure is central, use common knowledge rather than automatically adding supernatural unease. Stronger graphic cruelty is not a prerequisite for fear supervision.
- Basis: S12's connection between characterization and fear, plus common character and causality knowledge.

## Comparison, images, and trial reads

Keep the event constant and compare one difference, such as when the full appearance is shown, how sound is conveyed, or who notices the anomaly. In PNGs, check clues, spatial relationships, and order of changes at publication size. Do not assert effects of black coverage or expressions without actually viewing the images.

In trial reads, first ask what seemed wrong and when it was noticed. Separate mere confusion from fear. Do not combine shock, disgust, and unease as one response. Follow [Common supervision procedures](../routing.md) to record acceptance and costs. Comparative verification with actual project PNGs and readers has not been performed.
