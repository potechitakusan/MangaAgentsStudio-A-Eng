# Supervision: Affection, Embarrassment, and Budding Romance

Status: Experimental. Updated / sources checked: 2026-09-19. Covers feeling that someone is special, awareness of that feeling, and how it is communicated. These are **production proposals** informed by [S16](../sources.md#s16) and [S04](../sources.md#s04), not diagnoses of romantic feelings or demonstrated reader effects.

For a moment of being captivated by someone's appeal, see [Non-explicit allure](../allure/README.md); for mutual reliance, see [Trust](../trust/README.md). Use only needed items in scenes intended to be romantic. For general character and viewpoint matters, see [Common knowledge](../../manga/05-story-character.md).

<a id="l01"></a>

## L01 Separate who feels affection from stages of awareness and communication

- Examine: Who cares about whom? Do personal awareness, the other's understanding, and information given to readers coincide?
- Use when: A gaze or blush is treated as proof of mutual love or an established relationship.
- Compare: Reveal one person's thoughts, leave a hint through behavior alone, or show the recipient's interpretation in a separate reaction.
- Costs and exceptions: Revealing thoughts reduces ambiguity. Embarrassment can also follow failure or attention; alone, it is not proof of romance.
- Basis: Organizing information stages from S16's approach to preserving one person's romantic feelings.

<a id="l02"></a>

## L02 Show a choice that changes because it is this particular person

- Examine: Does continuing a conversation, remembering something, or slightly changing plans have a character-specific reason?
- Use when: Kindness offered to everyone is meant to convey special affection.
- Compare: Contrast the usual response to others, connect an earlier conversation to action, or show a small hesitation and revised choice.
- Costs and exceptions: Excessive special treatment can break work responsibilities, promises, or personality. Explicit affection and a stage where behavior has not yet changed are also valid.
- Basis: S16's everyday exchanges and common knowledge of desire and action.

<a id="l03"></a>

## L03 Match embarrassment to character voice

- Examine: What feeling is being concealed, and how does behavior differ from normal? Do shouting, silence, or increased politeness fit the person?
- Use when: Blushing, denial, and averted gazes are added to everyone.
- Compare: Rephrase, return to a familiar task, show straightforward delight, or deflect with a joke.
- Costs and exceptions: If aggression used to hide embarrassment hurts someone, preserve that impact. Omitting blushing is not mandatory.
- Basis: Applying S04's varied reactions to established characters. Do not infer true feelings conclusively from a reaction.

<a id="l04"></a>

## L04 Narrow down what changed through the exchange

- Examine: What is this step: a form of address, a topic, or a reason to meet again?
- Use when: A romantic event leaves no change in the relationship, or advances it too abruptly.
- Compare: Leave an opening for the next conversation, learn just one thing, or communicate affection while deferring the reply.
- Costs and exceptions: Progress is not required every time. Reassuring repeated conversations or stalled unrequited affection may be intended.
- Basis: Adapting S16's accumulation of conversations. Do not treat only the pre-relationship stage as correct.

<a id="l05"></a>

## L05 Preserve the other person's reply and choice

- Examine: Can the recipient choose delight, uncertainty, refusal, reflection, or another response?
- Use when: A confession or approach omits the other's wishes or established relationship.
- Compare: Include a reply, let the person choose distance, or carry differing interpretations into the next exchange.
- Costs and exceptions: Overexplaining the reply reduces lingering feeling. Do not fill in deferral or refusal as hidden acceptance.
- Basis: This kit's proposal informed by S16 and common character knowledge.

## Comparison, images, and trial reads

Keep the person feeling affection and the relationship stage constant. Compare thoughts / gestures or openness / concealment. In PNGs, check whose viewpoint and reaction are shown and the order of approach and reply. In trial reads, ask who seemed to feel what toward whom and what changed this time. Comparative verification with actual project PNGs and readers has not been performed. Follow [Common procedures](../routing.md).
