# When and How to Use Supervision

In version 0.3.2, first decide what the current scene should convey, then select the themes needed below. Do not activate supervision solely from the work's overall genre.

| What the scene needs | Reference | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Develop the mechanism or mismatch behind a joke | [Comedy](gag/README.md) | Existing dedicated knowledge |
| Choose a response to unusual behavior | [Straight-man responses](tsukkomi/README.md) | Experimental; also useful for character and relationships outside comedy |
| Create awareness of another person without nudity or sexual acts | [Non-explicit allure](allure/README.md) | Experimental; distinguish appeal to readers from changes between characters |
| Show skill, judgment, resolve, or differences in stature | [Coolness and stature](stature/README.md) | Experimental; distinguish strength, likability, and the correctness of beliefs |
| Convey foreboding or fear of a threat | [Unease and fear](horror/README.md) | Experimental; distinguish lack of understanding from fear |
| Convey the issue behind anger and the relationship change | [Anger, arguments, and rupture](conflict/README.md) | Experimental; distinguish argument intensity from rupture |
| Depict absence and a person's life afterward | [Grief, loss, and aftermath](grief/README.md) | Experimental; distinguish the end of a scene from the end of a feeling |
| Show special affection and how it is communicated | [Affection, embarrassment, and budding romance](romance/README.md) | Experimental; distinguish one person's affection from a mutually established relationship |
| Show entrusting, relying, and being together | [Intimacy and trust](trust/README.md) | Experimental; distinguish liking, areas of trust, and obligation |
| Depict the gap between intended words and reception | [Awkwardness and misunderstanding](awkwardness/README.md) | Experimental; distinguish confirmed facts from interpretation |
| Convey the urgency of a present choice or remaining opportunity | [Crisis, anxiety, and urgency](urgency/README.md) | Experimental; distinguish uncertainty about the ending from tension during events |
| Change the interpretation of events through new information | [Surprise, discovery, and revelation](revelation/README.md) | Experimental; distinguish reader surprise from a character's discovery |
| Show changed circumstances and what is rewarded | [Exhilaration, reversal, and release](payoff/README.md) | Experimental; distinguish victory from release, and achievement from luck |
| Choose reactions that make readers care about a character | [Endearing qualities and attachment](endearment/README.md) | Experimental; distinguish attachment from assessment of ability or behavior |
| Choose a next move in response to the other person | [Bargaining and probing](bargaining/README.md) | Experimental; distinguish knowledge, inference, and bluffing |
| Choose relationships and ways of living after hurt | [Reconciliation, forgiveness, and solace](reconciliation/README.md) | Experimental; distinguish apology, forgiveness, and resuming a relationship |
| Other emotions, causality, and readability | [Common knowledge](../review-workflow.md#common-knowledge) | Do not invent missing specialist knowledge and claim it is already supported |

Do not choose allure supervision merely because a relationship or physical distance is close. For family habits or friendship, consider trust; for special affection, romance; for respect for skill or resolve, stature. Select by the scene's purpose. If none of the covered effects applies, dedicated supervision may be out of scope.

1. Confirm the central effect, accompanying elements, and the characterization, emotion, and settings to preserve.
2. Read only necessary items from the selected themes. Consult [Sources](sources.md) when checking source claims, and compare applicability, alternatives, and exceptions.
3. Return to [Common knowledge](../review-workflow.md#common-knowledge) for character consistency, causality, knowledge differences, reading order, and endings.
4. In written scripts, compare expressive options and their costs. After PNGs exist, actually open the target images and check whether they convey the intent.
5. The main agent decides acceptance, then presents accepted revisions again for confirmation.

## Combining themes

Preserve the work's required effects, such as combat causality and tension over winning or losing in a battle, or both people's desires and choices in romance. Do not erase ability conditions for a joke or omit a reply to a serious proposal. If comedy is not primary, increasing humor is not a goal in itself.

For example, if the main effect is a moment of becoming aware of another person, compare a straight-man response only as needed for concealed embarrassment or character voice. Do not automatically turn appeal to readers into an established romance within the story. Do not maximize every theme's effect; retain the option of no change in light of the main effect.

For the arrival of a powerful figure, start with stature if admiration is primary or horror if an inescapable threat is primary. An angry face may still call for straight-man response guidance if its central function is comedy; if anger persists, examine the dispute and relationship change. When anger mixes with grief, preserve both the main effect and needed responses instead of reducing them to one emotion. Do not combine allure, coolness, and intimacy into one scale of appeal.

Choose urgency for pressure around future outcomes or choices, revelation for changed interpretation through information, and payoff for changed circumstances and gains. Choose awkwardness for the gap between intent and reception, bargaining for a move intended to influence another person, and reconciliation for relationships chosen after conflict. Do not select a theme from an expression or silence alone or automatically apply every listed theme.

State the themes and items used and the scope actually examined. The 15 added themes are experimental; general reader effects are unverified. Check with each project's PNGs and trial reads, and do not label a text-only assessment as image review.

## What to return

- The target version, location, and scope actually examined.
- Facts confirmed in the script or image, and hypotheses about reader interpretation.
- Proposed changes, clues to provide, expected effects, and effects that might be lost.
- Kit documents and items consulted, unverified matters, and acceptance decisions for the main agent.

Do not record an agent's assessment as an actual reader response. Do not introduce new scores or weights. Use the project's `docs/reviews/SUPERVISION.md` when needed.
