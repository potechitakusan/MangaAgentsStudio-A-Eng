# Supervision: Intimacy and Trust

Status: Experimental. Updated / sources checked: 2026-09-19. Covers entrusting, relying, and being together. These are **production proposals** informed by [S17](../sources.md#s17) and [S16](../sources.md#s16). They apply to friendship, family, work, and romance without changing the type of relationship.

For romantic feelings, see [Affection](../romance/README.md); for choosing a relationship again after damage, see [Reconciliation](../reconciliation/README.md). For roles and actions, see [Character](../../manga/05-story-character.md); for habits and gestures, [Scenes](../../manga/04-scene-dialogue-props.md).

<a id="f01"></a>

## F01 Decide what is trusted and how much is entrusted

- Examine: Is the trust in skills, promises, secrets, or judgment? Does it coincide with liking or closeness?
- Use when: One act of cooperation becomes trust in everything about the person.
- Compare: Entrust only work, confide only a secret, or rely on an area of expertise.
- Costs and exceptions: Even an enemy or disliked person may be trusted on a particular promise. The entire scope need not be explained in dialogue.
- Basis: Applying S17's approach to a trustworthy enemy.

<a id="f02"></a>

## F02 Connect an entrusted action to its reception

- Examine: Who entrusted what, and how did the other respond? Was it freely chosen or required by rules, duty, or coercion? Is praise substituting for the relationship?
- Use when: People call each other teammates without a visible reason they need each other.
- Compare: Assign a role, report a failure, ask for help, or reliably complete the requested scope.
- Costs and exceptions: A procedural handoff alone does not prove personal trust. Do not make someone incompetent to create a rescue opportunity. Trusting at first meeting can also be depicted as a personal judgment.
- Basis: S17 and common knowledge of choice and causality.

<a id="f03"></a>

## F03 Preserve small responses in shared habits

- Examine: What do procedures, names, handoffs, and silence mean for this pair?
- Use when: Physical proximity or silence alone is meant to signal intimacy.
- Compare: Naturally adapt to the other's routine, notice an unusual tone, or keep working without forcing conversation.
- Costs and exceptions: Trust need not involve shared habits. Silence alone also permits readings of tension or rejection; examine the surrounding material.
- Basis: Extending S16's everyday exchanges to nonromantic relationships is this kit's hypothesis.

<a id="f04"></a>

## F04 Preserve differences and room to refuse

- Examine: How are the other's limits treated when opinions differ or a request is declined?
- Use when: Trust is represented by agreeing to everything or revealing every secret.
- Compare: Explain and offer an alternative, delegate unfamiliar areas, or accept that something need not be discussed now.
- Costs and exceptions: Do not correct dependence or a one-sided relationship when intentional. Compare readings while preserving the other's burden.
- Basis: Applying S17's comments on scope and distance to fictional relationships.

<a id="f05"></a>

## F05 Separate changed trust from remaining distance

- Examine: After shared success or failure, what can newly be entrusted and what still cannot?
- Use when: One rescue or alliance erases all prior distrust or hurt.
- Compare: Add a small request, continue only the shared role, or express thanks while keeping distance.
- Costs and exceptions: Large relationship changes can work. What matters is supporting the degree of change through the story's events.
- Basis: S17, earlier anger supervision, and common character knowledge.

## Comparison, images, and trial reads

Keep the relationship type and request constant. Compare explanation / handoff, or agreement / responses that allow disagreement. In PNGs, examine recipients, reactions, and continued roles. In trial reads, ask what can be entrusted in this relationship and where distance remains. Comparative verification with actual project PNGs and readers has not been performed.
