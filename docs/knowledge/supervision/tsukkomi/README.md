# Supervision: Straight-Man Responses and Reactions to the Unusual

Added: 2026-09-19. Experimental. Use for written-script consultation and image review when choosing responses to odd behavior or remarks. Also useful outside primarily comic scenes when reactions convey character or relationships. The stable ID `tsukkomi` refers to the Japanese straight-man response role.

See the [Source register](../sources.md) for creators' and editors' views, [Comedy](../gag/README.md) for premises and mismatches, and [Common knowledge](../../review-workflow.md#common-knowledge) for character, performance, and information order. T01–T06 below are conditional production proposals from this kit, not rules guaranteeing humor or likability.

## Decide first

Confirm the main effect, the responder's goal, the pair's relationship, and what readers already know. Infer vocabulary, length, response intensity, and directness from existing dialogue and performance. Do not default to loud voices, short lines, or correcting everything.

<a id="t01"></a>

## T01 Whose standard makes what unusual?

- Examine: Does the act depart from the story world's norms, a personal fixation, or reader expectations? Does the responder have reason to know and care?
- Use when: The action is visible but it is unclear who objects to what.
- Compare: Correct it, be troubled, or focus on another issue. Choose from differences in knowledge and interests.
- Costs and exceptions: Making every observation conventionally sensible erases individual biases. Do not abruptly call an established normal behavior abnormal for authorial convenience.
- Basis: [S01](../sources.md#s01) on character positions, combined with existing knowledge about character and information differences. The reaction categories are production proposals.

<a id="t02"></a>

## T02 Responses that aid understanding versus responses that add meaning

- Examine: Separate facts already clear from images or earlier dialogue from judgments, emotions, and comparisons supplied only by the response.
- Use when: Explanation repeats, or removing the response makes the event unclear.
- Compare: Shorten restatements, preserve a character-specific judgment, or add visual evidence of the subject or result.
- When reducing text, check that replacement clues are readable at publication size. Without images, leave this unverified.
- Costs and exceptions: Preserve long phrasing or a rapid verbal barrage when that is the attraction. Do not remove needed correction or character voice merely for brevity.
- Basis: [S02](../sources.md#s02) informs dialogue selection. Distinguishing duplication from performance is a production proposal.

<a id="t03"></a>

## T03 Compare speaking, silence, and letting it pass

| Choice | Hypothesized useful conditions | Clues to provide | Potential loss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Verbal correction | The specific mismatch needs clarification | The target and character-specific language | Readers' enjoyment of noticing it themselves |
| Silent response | The subject is clear and expression or movement conveys the response | Gaze, a stopped action, the next choice | Verbal charm and clarity of meaning |
| Letting familiar behavior pass | Experience with the recurring eccentricity is established | Anticipatory handling and continued ordinary tasks | Surprise and first-time-reader understanding |
| Joining or amplifying | The next person also has desires or biases | The connection between original and new mismatches | A point of comparison with ordinary behavior |
| Leaving it to readers | Readers can notice the anomaly without character reactions | A visible difference between premise and result | Interpretive certainty and character interplay |

Absent reactions alone do not establish that the effect has successfully been left to readers. [S02](../sources.md#s02) is a reference for reconsidering form; do not attribute this table to its creator.

<a id="t04"></a>

## T04 Preserve character voice and reaction order

- Examine: When does the character notice, and what are they protecting by speaking? Beyond speech style, do interests, knowledge, hesitation, and behavior distinguish characters?
- Use when: Everyone reacts alike, a correction suddenly sounds unlike the character, or several people repeat the same surprise.
- Compare: Return vocabulary or focus to personal interests, let someone notice later, or continue the task instead of speaking. Preserve a reason when departing from ordinary behavior.
- Costs and exceptions: Naturalizing a response can remove intended excess. Do not fix response order or the number of pause panels.
- Basis: [S04](../sources.md#s04) and common knowledge of performance and information order.

<a id="t05"></a>

## T05 Examine the impression a response creates of character and relationship

- Examine: Does it criticize a particular behavior or reject the whole person? From the recipient's next action, decide whether the intended effect is closeness, resistance, or hurt.
- Use when: Supposedly friendly banter may read as one-sided aggression.
- Compare: Specify the target, let the recipient answer back, or add an action that helps with the task.
- Costs and exceptions: Softening is not the goal when hostility or biting humor is primary. Leave room for different readings by readers familiar with the relationship and first-time readers.
- Basis: An impression hypothesis informed by [S01](../sources.md#s01), not a measurement of likability.

<a id="t06"></a>

## T06 Choose where to acknowledge other emotions

- Examine: Does humor cut off a confession, apology, loss, or crisis before its essential point receives a response?
- Use when: Added humor makes a character's desire or the scene's resolution unreadable.
- Compare: Put the necessary answer first, use a small response that preserves tension, or omit the joke.
- Costs and exceptions: Not answering can itself matter when depicting concealed embarrassment or avoidance. Check that the unresolved issue is readable.
- Basis: [S03](../sources.md#s03) and existing judgments about main effects and endings.

## Feedback and verification

Connect target location / observation / interpretation hypothesis / T number / proposed change / effects preserved and lost. Return a no-change option when there is no problem. If evidence is weak, present alternatives as differences of taste.

In PNGs, inspect the actual target, reaction direction, and dialogue reading order. With only a written script, visual aspects remain unverified. In trial reads, first ask what seemed odd and how the pair's relationship was understood. Treat understanding and funniness separately. Return to [Common review](../../review-workflow.md).
