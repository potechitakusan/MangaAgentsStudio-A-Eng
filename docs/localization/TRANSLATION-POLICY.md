# Translation and adaptation policy

Use natural US English without reducing the source to a summary. Preserve requirements, exceptions, permission boundaries, experimental status, limitations, and historical source dates. Translate examples for the same narrative purpose. Do not turn historical product observations into current recommendations.

US edition defaults: left-to-right panels within each row, rows top to bottom, balloons in the same reading order, horizontal word-wrapped English, centered speech-balloon text, normal English punctuation. Captions may be left aligned. Keep an explicit Japanese mode where implemented. Recompose panels and timing effects without mirroring artwork. Print trim sizes remain optional project choices; the web-first workflow has no mandatory US print dimensions.

Keep product names, machine identifiers, API fields, file paths, source URLs, attribution, and license originals stable. Legacy fields ending `_ja` and Japanese compatibility fixtures may remain where necessary; explain their scope instead of misrepresenting them as English. Third-party Japanese polishing skills remain optional, disabled, and intended for Japanese source text.

Source identification is an explicit exception to English prose. Preserve the Japanese baseline's citation labels, article and book titles, credited names, publishers, and source identity records in their original language. Do not replace them with translated titles or guessed romanizations. Originally English source titles remain English. Surrounding explanations of claims, applicability, and limitations remain US English; URLs and historical dates stay unchanged. The owner's 2026-10-03 clarification also applies to future updates.

Bundled example pages are localized in their images as well as their descriptions. Preserve character identity, Japanese settings, visual clues, silent beats, and the original uncertainty. Move whole panels without reflecting their contents; reposition balloons to preserve question/answer order when speaker positions stay fixed. Wordless pages may retain their originals after checking LTR continuity. Original production history and external Japanese editions remain explicitly labeled. Record generated-art differences and provisional name readings in the example record and owner questionnaire. Never claim that changing a caption translates embedded image lettering.

Record intentional cultural changes separately from accidental semantic drift in the independent review. Preserve ambiguities using an explicit working decision, and put questions to the owner in `HEARING-SHEET.ja.md` without delaying the completed English implementation.
